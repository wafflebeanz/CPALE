🌸 Fundamentals

Economics & Scarcity

Economics: Efficient allocation of scarce resources. Choices are necessary due to scarcity (unlimited wants but only limited available resources).

Microeconomics: Deals with economic behavior of individual units or specific segment of the whole economy. Study of how individual choices are influenced by economic force.

Kinds of Valuable Economic Goods

  • Consumer Goods: Direct satisfaction of consumers. No need for further processing.
  • Capital Goods: Indirect satisfaction. Machine and equipment needed to produce consumer goods.
  • Essential Goods: Needed for survival (Food, clothing, shelter).
  • Luxury Goods: Non-essential items that improve lifestyle.
  • Economic Goods: Anything valuable (we're willing to pay for it). Opposite of free (public) goods which are not paid for and provided by the government (e.g., air, sea water).

📉 Market Mechanics

Theory of Demand and Supply

Demand

Quantity of commodities which buyers are willing and able to purchase at a given price, time, and place.

LAW OF DEMAND: Higher Price = Lower Quantity Demanded (Ceteris Paribus). Inverse relationship. Downward sloping curve.
  • Substitution Effect: Buyers look for cheaper alternatives.
  • Income Effect: Less buyers will afford such price.
  • Curve Shift: Changes due to factors other than product's own price (Right = Increase, Left = Decrease).

Supply

Quantity of commodities which producers are willing and able to produce and offer at a given price, place, and time.

LAW OF SUPPLY: Higher Price = Higher Quantity Supplied. Direct relationship. Upward sloping curve.
  • Suppliers want to take advantage of higher prices for profit.
  • Curve Shift: Changes due to factors other than product's own price (Right = Increase, Left = Decrease).

⚖️️ Equilibrium

Market Equilibrium

State of supply and demand balance. Equilibrium Price (Market-clearing price): Quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.

  • Disequilibrium: S and D do not meet.
  • Stable Equilibrium: System returns after small disturbance (e.g., Jeep Fare & Gasoline).
  • Unstable Equilibrium: System moves away after disturbance.
  • Walrasian Equilibrium: Changes in prices maintain stable equilibrium (e.g., Coke at 1.5L bottles).
  • Marshallian Equilibrium: Changes in quantity maintain stable equilibrium (e.g., Pandesal at 2 pesos, but size varies).
  • Surplus: Excess supply ($S > D$). Reaches price floor -> price decreases.
  • Shortage: Excess demand ($D > S$). Reaches price ceiling -> price increases.

Surplus Formulas

Consumer Surplus: Willingness to Pay Price - Market Price

$$ \frac{1}{2} \times Q_{Eq} \times (\text{Max Price} - \text{Eq Price}) $$

Production Surplus: Market Selling Price - Economic Cost

Total Surplus: Consumer Surplus + Production Surplus

Consumers are willing to pay for a glass of milk tea for 120 pesos but found that it has a market value of only 110 pesos. Total equilibrium quantity is 600,000 units and supply is 700,000 units. How much is consumer surplus?

Formula:1/2 × Q(eq) × (Max - Eq Price)
Plug in:1/2 × 600,000 × (120 - 110)
Consumer Surplus:₱3,000,000
📈 Elasticity

Demand Elasticities

Price Elasticity of Demand ($E_D$)

$$ E_D = \frac{\% \Delta \text{ Quantity Demanded}}{\% \Delta \text{ Price}} $$

  • $E_D > 1$: Elastic (Reacts MORE. TR moves opposite to Price. Luxuries).
  • $E_D < 1$: Inelastic (Reacts LESS. TR moves directly with Price. Necessities).
  • $E_D = 1$: Unitary (Proportionate. No TR effect).
  • $E_D = 0$: Perfectly Inelastic (NO reaction).

Income Elasticity

$$ E_I = \frac{\% \Delta \text{ Quantity Demanded}}{\% \Delta \text{ Consumer Income}} $$

Positive = Normal Good, Negative = Inferior Good, $E_I > 1$ = Luxury.

Cross-Elasticity

$$ E_C = \frac{\% \Delta \text{ Qty Demanded (Product X)}}{\% \Delta \text{ Price (Product Y)}} $$

$E_C > 0$ = Substitute, $E_C < 0$ = Complement, $E_C = 0$ = Unrelated.

A local car dealership gathered data. Real income falls from 5M to 4M, demand plummets from 10,000 to 5,000 units sold. Compute $E_D$.

% Δ Qty:(10k - 5k) / 10k = 0.50
% Δ Income:(5M - 4M) / 5M = 0.20
E_D (Income):0.50 / 0.20 = 2.50
🏭 Firms & Production

Production Costs & Market Structures

Costs & Returns

  • Law of Diminishing Returns: Adding an additional factor of production results in smaller increases in output (MC decreases then increases).
  • Short-run Cost: At least one input FIXED (e.g., Materials).
  • Long-run Cost: All are variable (e.g., Fixed Asset acquired).
  • Economies of Scale: Decline in ATC as firm expands (specialization).
  • Diseconomies of Scale: ATC rises as firm expands (inefficient coordination).

Profits

  • Accounting Profit: Total Revenue - Explicit Costs (actual expenses/sunk).
  • Economic Profit: Total Revenue - Explicit - Implicit Costs (opportunity costs).
  • Normal Profit: Economic Profit is ZERO (Economic Breakeven). Average industry return.
Implicit: 200k, Explicit: 1M. Sold 4,000 units at 300.
Acctg = (4k * 300) - 1M = 200,000
Econ = (4k * 300) - 1M - 200k = 0

Decision-Making

  • Short-run: Operate if $TR > TVC$. Shutdown if $TR < TVC$.
  • Marginal Approach: Maximize profit where $MR = MC$.
  • If $MR > MC$, Increase Production.
  • If $MR < MC$, Decrease Production.
  • Minimum Profit formula: $(MR-MC) \times Q - TFC$

📊 National Income

GDP & GNP

Economic Growth: Self-sustaining reasonable level. Development: Improving quality of life.

Gross Domestic Product (GDP)

Total value of goods/services produced WITHIN a country (Gawa Dito Pinas). Excludes OFW income, intermediate goods, illegal activities.

Expenditure Approach: $$ GDP = C + I + G + NX $$

  • C: Household, I: Business, G: Govt, NX: Net Exports.
Real GDP = Nominal GDP adjusted for inflation (constant prices)
GDP Deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) * 100

Gross National Product (GNP)

Value of goods/services produced by a country's citizens globally (Gawa Ng Pinoy). Includes OFW remittances, excludes foreigners in PH.

🔄 Cycles & Multipliers

The Multiplier & Business Cycle

The Multiplier

Magnified expected increase in income per peso of investment.

$$ \text{Multiplier} = \frac{1}{1 - \text{MPC}} = \frac{1}{\text{MPS}} $$

1. Decline in spending 30B. MPC 75%. Expected GDP decline?

Multiplier = 1 / (1 - 0.75) = 4
GDP Decline = 30B * 4 = 120 Billion

2. 0.5 MPS. Shift AD rightwards by 80B. Govt spending increase needed?

Multiplier = 1 / 0.5 = 2
80B = Govt * 2 -> Govt = 40 Billion

Business Cycle

  • Peak: Highest point, marks end of expansion.
  • Recession: From peak to trough. Falling GDP for 2 successive quarters (6 mos).
  • Trough: Lowest point, marks end of recession.
  • Expansion (Recovery): Trough to peak. Rising GDP.
  • Depression: Prolonged, severe recession.
🔥 Economic Issues

Inflation & Unemployment

Inflation

Sustained increase in prices. Weakening of purchasing power.

  • Demand-Pull: Excessive AD (Too many people want to buy).
  • Cost-Push: Increased production cost (Supply side).
  • Stagflation: High inflation + High unemployment + Stagnant growth.
  • Rule of 70: $ \text{Doubling Time} = \frac{70 \text{ years}}{\text{Growth Rate (\%)}}$

Measures of Inflation:

CPI: Consumers. PPI: Producers. WPI: Wholesale.

$$ \text{Inflation (\%)} = \frac{\text{CY CPI} - \text{Base Year CPI}}{\text{Base Year CPI}} \times 100 $$

Unemployment

Inversely related to inflation (Phillips Curve) and Real GDP.

  • Frictional: Entering workforce, between jobs.
  • Structural: Skill/demand mismatch, tech replacement (AI).
  • Seasonal: Predictable (farmers in winter).
  • Cyclical: Economic downturns/recessions.

Natural Unemployment Rate (NUR):

= Frictional + Structural

= JSR / (JSR + JFR)

Okun's Law: 1% drop in employment = 2% drop in potential GDP.

1. Underemployment Rate
Formula: Underemployed / Total Employed
= 7M / (25M Full + 7M Part + 15M Business)
= 7M / 47M = 14.89%
2. Job Separation Rate (JSR)
Given NUR = 5%, JFR = 19%
0.05 = JSR / (JSR + 0.19)
0.05 JSR + 0.0095 = JSR
0.95 JSR = 0.0095 -> JSR = 1%
3. Rule of 70
Price doubled between 2005 and 2025 (20 yrs).
20 yrs = 70 yrs / Growth Rate
Growth Rate = 70 / 20 = 3.5%
4. Okun's Law Effect
5% increase in unemployment rate.
5 * 2% = 10% drop in Potential GDP
🏛️ Macro Policies

Fiscal & Monetary Policy

Fiscal Policy

Govt adjusts spending levels and tax rates. Goal: Economic Growth. Supervised by President (short-term, political).

  • Expansion: Increase deficit (Increase spending, decrease tax).
  • Contraction: Decrease deficit (Increase tax, decrease spending).

Monetary Policy

Central bank (BSP) influences money supply and interest rates. Goal: Stability. (Long-term, non-political).

  • Loose (Expansionary): Lower interest rates, increase money supply. Fights recession. Floods market with peso.
  • Tight (Contractionary): Higher interest rates, decrease money supply. Fights inflation. Reduces production.

Economic Theories

  • Classical: Market equilibrium fixes itself (no govt intervention).
  • Keynesian: Focuses on Fiscal Policy.
  • Monetary: Focuses on Monetary Policy.
  • Neo-Keynesian: Combined Fiscal and Monetary.
  • Supply-Side: Increasing ability to supply goods stimulates growth.
🌐 Global Trade

Trade & Foreign Exchange

Foreign Currency

  • Direct Quote: Domestic per 1 Foreign (e.g., P50 for $1).
  • Indirect Quote: Foreign per 1 Domestic (e.g., $0.02 for P1). Lower rate = weak domestic currency.
  • Spot Rate: Immediate delivery.
  • Forward Rate: Future exchange.
  • Cross-Currency: Neither is USD (e.g., CAD/JPY).
  • Appreciation: Stronger currency, cheaper imports, bad for domestic producers.
  • Depreciation: Weaker currency, cheaper domestic goods, good for exports/employment.

International Trade

  • Comparative Advantage: Lower opportunity cost.
  • Absolute Advantage: Greater volume/efficiency.
  • Free Trade vs Protectionism: Laissez-faire vs Tariffs/Quotas/Subsidies to protect local markets.
  • Financing Trade: Banker's Acceptance (guaranteed), Countertrade (Barter, Counter Purchase, Offset), Forfaiting, Factoring.
Free Rider Problem: Burden on shared resource by those who don't pay.
Mitigation: Govt tax, privatizing the resource, or imposing a small user fee.
