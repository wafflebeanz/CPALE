Nature of Assurance Engagement

An engagement in which a practitioner expresses a conclusion designed to enhance the degree of confidence of the intended users (other than the responsible party) about the outcome of the evaluation or measurement of a subject matter against criteria.

Objective: Improve the quality and credibility of information for decision-making. Independence is always required.

Types of Assurance Engagements

1. Reasonable Assurance (Audit)

  • Reduces engagement risk to an acceptably low level.
  • Provides a positive form of conclusion (e.g., "In our opinion...").
  • Provides high, but not absolute, assurance.
Absolute assurance is unattainable due to UNFIT factors: Use of judgment, Nature of subject matter, Fact that evidence is persuasive not conclusive, Inherent limitations of internal control, Testing sample data.

2. Limited Assurance (Review)

  • Reduces engagement risk to a level that is acceptable but greater than reasonable assurance.
  • Provides a negative form of conclusion (e.g., "Nothing has come to our attention...").
  • Provides moderate assurance.

Assurance Engagement Risk Workflow

The risk that the practitioner expresses an inappropriate conclusion when the subject matter information is materially misstated.

Risk Component Formula ASSURANCE ENGAGEMENT RISK (AER)
= Risk of Material Misstatement (RMM) * Detection Risk (DR)

Where RMM breaks down into:
[INHERENT RISK] : Susceptibility assuming no controls
      +
[CONTROL RISK]  : Risk controls won't prevent/detect

Note: In a limited assurance engagement, the level of assurance engagement risk is higher due to less extensive evidence gathering.

Elements of an Assurance Engagement

All five elements must be present: CPA3Y

Sequential Engagement Flow 1. [C] Criteria setup (RUN CR)
2. [P] Proper Subject Matter defined
3. [A] Appropriate/Sufficient Evidence gathered
4. [3] 3-Party Relationship established
5. [Y] Yield a Written Report
  • Criteria (Benchmarks): Must be RUN CR: Relevant, Understandable, Neutral, Complete, Reliable. Can be established (laws/PFRS) or specifically developed.
  • Subject Matter: Can be financial performance, non-financial conditions, physical characteristics, systems/processes, or behavior. Must be identifiable and capable of consistent evaluation.
  • Evidence: Planned with Professional Skepticism. Must evaluate Sufficiency (quantity, affected by risk) and Appropriateness (quality/relevance/reliability).
  • 3-Party Relationship: 1) Practitioner (CPA), 2) Responsible Party (owns subject matter), 3) Intended Users (relies on report).
  • Written Report: Conveys the reasonable (positive) or limited (negative) conclusion.

Acceptance Requisites

A practitioner only accepts an engagement if preliminary knowledge indicates:

  • Relevant ethical requirements (independence, competence) will be satisfied.
  • The subject matter is appropriate.
  • Criteria are suitable and available to users.
  • Practitioner has access to sufficient appropriate evidence.
  • There is a rational purpose for the engagement.
Trade-off restriction: Once accepted, a practitioner may not change an engagement to a non-assurance engagement, or from reasonable to limited, without reasonable justification.

Nature of Non-Assurance

Engagements that do not result in the practitioner's expression of a conclusion providing any level of assurance. The primary purpose is to provide advice and technical assistance to help a client conduct business more effectively.

Key Rule: Independence is not required for non-assurance engagements.

Reports & Association Rules

When issuing reports for non-assurance engagements, the practitioner must strictly avoid misleading users:

  • Should not use the words "assurance", "audit", or "review".
  • Should not imply compliance with assurance standards (PSAs, PSREs, PSAEs).
Practitioner Association: A CPA is associated when reporting on information or consenting to use their name. If a client inappropriately uses the CPA's name, the CPA must demand cessation and consider legal advice or informing third parties.
