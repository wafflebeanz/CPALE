Topic 6: Business Combinations (PFRS 3) Basics & Acquisition Method

PFRS 3 Scope

📖 Scope & Core Concept

A Business Combination is a transaction or other event in which an acquirer obtains control of one or more businesses. Governed by PFRS 3 (Business Combinations), which mandates the Acquisition Method (referred to as the Purchase Method under PFRS for SMEs).

💡
Crucial Control Test: Control exists when the investor has exposure or rights to variable returns from its involvement with the investee and has the ability to affect those returns through its power over the relevant activities (PFRS 10).
Mandatory Steps

🐾 The 4 Steps of the Acquisition Method

  • Step 1: Identify the Acquirer → The combining entity that obtains control of the acquiree (controls the relevant activities).
  • Step 2: Determine the Acquisition Date → The date on which the acquirer officially obtains control of the acquiree.
  • Step 3: Recognize & Measure Identifiable Assets, Liabilities & NCI → Measured at their Fair Values at the acquisition date.
  • Step 4: Recognize & Measure Goodwill or Gain from Bargain Purchase:
    • If Total Consideration or Aggregate is greater than FVINA → Goodwill (Asset).
    • If Total Consideration or Aggregate is less than FVINA → Gain from Bargain Purchase (credited to Profit/Loss immediately).

Stock Acquisition, NCI Measurement & Goodwill Allocation

Control Structure

🏰 Stock Acquisition Framework

Results in a Parent (Acquirer) and Subsidiary (Acquiree) relationship, recognizing a Non-Controlling Interest (NCI) when the parent acquires less than 100% of voting shares.

Aggregate Amount = Total Consideration + NCI + Fair Value of Previously Held Equity Instrument (FV-PHEI)
Goodwill / (Gain on BP) = Aggregate Amount − FVINA
NCI Valuation

🧸 Measurement of NCI at Date of Acquisition

Option 1 Fair Value (Assumed if Silent)
  • Must NOT be below Proportionate Share.
  • Given Fair Value OR Assumed Fair Value:
    Assumed FV of NCI = [(Total Consideration − Control Premium) / Controlling Interest %] × NCI %
  • Results in Full Goodwill (allocated to both CI and NCI).
Option 2 Proportionate Share (Minimum Amount)
  • NCI at Proportionate Share = FVINA × NCI %
  • Results in Partial Goodwill (allocated 100% to CI; NCI has zero goodwill).
💡
Gain from Bargain Purchase Rule: If the transaction results in a Gain from Bargain Purchase, it is ALWAYS 100% allocated to the Controlling Interest (CI)!
Step Acquisitions & SMEs

🔍 Measuring FV-PHEI & SME Differences

FV of Previously Held Equity Instrument (FV-PHEI):

In a step acquisition, the previous investment is remeasured to Fair Value at DOA:

Assumed FV-PHEI = [Total Consideration / % Newly Acquired] × PHEI %
(No need to consider or deduct the control premium!)
Remeasurement Gain / (Loss) in P/L = FV-PHEI at DOA − Carrying Amount of PHEI at DOA
Key Differences for PFRS for SMEs:
  • Only Partial Goodwill is allowed (Full Goodwill is strictly prohibited!).
  • NCI is ALWAYS measured at Proportionate Share.

Essential Consolidated Formulas: Sales, COGS, Inventory & Gross Profit

💡
Master Consolidation Rationale: The consolidated group is treated as a single economic entity. All intra-group transfers (intercompany sales, purchases, and unrealized markup) must be eliminated so financial statements reflect only transactions with external third parties!
Formula 1

🏷️ 1. Consolidated Sales (Conso Sales)

Consolidated Sales = Parent Sales + Subsidiary Sales − Intercompany Sales
  • Intercompany Sales: The total billing price of all merchandise sold between parent and subsidiary during the period (both upstream and downstream).
  • Why Deducted? To eliminate internal double counting. Only sales to outside third parties represent genuine consolidated revenue.
Formula 2

📦 2. Consolidated Inventory (Conso Inventory)

Consolidated Inventory = Parent Ending Inventory + Subsidiary Ending Inventory − UPEI + Unamortized Excess FV of Inventory
  • UPEI (Unrealized Profit in Ending Inventory): The markup on intercompany goods still sitting in the buyer's warehouse at year-end. Deducted to reduce inventory back to original consolidated cost.
  • Unamortized Excess FV: Added if acquired subsidiary inventory had a fair value higher than book value at DOA and remains unsold to outsiders at year-end.
Formula 3

📉 3. Consolidated Cost of Goods Sold (Conso COGS)

Consolidated COGS = Parent COGS + Subsidiary COGS − Intercompany Sales + UPEI − RPBI + Amortization of Undervalued Inventory − Amortization of Overvalued Inventory
ComponentEffect on Conso COGSAccounting Reason
Less: Intercompany Sales − Deduct Reverses the duplicate cost recorded upon intra-group transfer.
Add: UPEI + Add Ending inventory was overstated by markup; lower ending inventory increases COGS!
Less: RPBI (Beg UPEI) − Deduct Beginning inventory was overstated by prior year's markup; lower beginning inventory decreases COGS!
Add: Undervalued Inv. Amort. + Add Additional acquisition cost recognized when acquiree's inventory is sold to external parties.
Formula 4

🍰 4. Consolidated Gross Profit (Conso GP)

Can be calculated using Two (2) Equivalent Perspectives:

Method A: Direct Top-Down Approach
Consolidated Gross Profit = Consolidated Sales − Consolidated COGS
Method B: Reconciliation from Separate Gross Profits
Consolidated GP = Parent GP + Subsidiary GP − UPEI + RPBI − Amortization of Undervalued Inventory + Amortization of Overvalued Inventory
💡
Notice that Intercompany Sales cancels out between Sales and COGS, proving that Method A and Method B always yield the exact same result!

Transaction Direction

🔀 Downstream vs. Upstream

Downstream (Parent → Subsidiary):

The Parent is the selling entity. All unrealized profits/losses and subsequent realizations are allocated 100% to the Controlling Interest (CI). NCI is completely unaffected (0%)!

Upstream (Subsidiary → Parent):

The Subsidiary is the selling entity. All unrealized profits/losses and subsequent realizations are shared proportionally between CI and NCI based on their respective percentages (CI % and NCI %).

Topic 11: Not-for-Profit Organizations (NPO) Basics

Fundamentals

🧸 Basic Characteristics of NPOs

Operated strictly for the benefit of society as a whole (other than for profit):

  • Cultural & Charitable
  • Civic & Social
  • Educational & Religious
💡
Key Distinctions:
  • Receives gratuitous considerations (donations), but can still receive onerous considerations (e.g., tuition, hospital bills, cafeteria sales).
  • No ownership interest: No stock shares, dividends, or residual equity holders.
Revenue Types

💎 Major Sources of Revenue

1 Onerous Considerations (Exchange)
  • Regular Sale of Goods or Services: Always classified as Unrestricted.
  • Investment Income: Usually classified as Unrestricted (unless the donor stipulated that earnings are restricted for a designated purpose).
2 Gratuitous Considerations (Non-Exchange)
  • Donations (termed Contribution Revenue).
  • May be classified as Unrestricted, Temporarily Restricted, or Permanently Restricted depending on donor instructions.
Scope & Rules

📚 Accounting Framework & Categories

Accounting Standards: Accrual basis under:

  • PFRS with the Conceptual Framework
  • US GAAP: SFAS 116 (Contributions Received and Made) and SFAS 117 (Financial Statements of NPOs)

4 Common Categories of NPOs:

  • 11.1. Voluntary Health and Welfare Organizations (VHWO): Primary source of revenues is exclusively from gratuitous considerations (donations from public).
  • 11.2. Hospitals and Other Health Care Organizations
  • 11.3. Colleges and Universities
  • 11.4. Other NPOs: Churches, Museums, Fraternities, Political Parties, etc.
Net Asset Classes

🍰 Composition of Net Assets or Funds

In NPO accounting, net assets take the place of owner's equity and are strictly grouped by donor restrictions:

🍦 Unrestricted Net Assets

  • No donor-imposed restrictions at all.
  • Includes resources whose use is within the Board of Trustees' (BOT) discretion.
  • Board-designated funds (quasi-endowments, internal reserves) are strictly Unrestricted because only external donors can impose valid restrictions!

⏳ Temporarily Restricted

  • Subject to donor-imposed restrictions as to:
    • Time: Available only in future periods or term endowment.
    • Purpose: For a specific project/asset purchase.
    • Happening of an event: Event occurrence.
  • Release Mechanism: Once the time elapses, the purpose is accomplished, or the event occurs, the amount is reclassified (transferred) to Unrestricted Net Assets.

🔒 Permanently Restricted

  • Subject to donor-imposed restrictions that do not expire and cannot be removed by the NPO.
  • Usually invested (Pure Endowment): The principal must remain untouchable in perpetuity, and only the investment interest/dividends are usable.

Pledges

💌 E. Pledges (Promises to Give)

Generally classified as Temporarily Restricted (due to time restriction). However, if pledged to be received within the current year without any purpose stipulations, it is Unrestricted.

Pledge Type Accounting Treatment
Unconditional Pledges Recognized as Contribution Revenue immediately at the time the pledge was made (when the pledge became receivable).

Special Funds

🌱 F. Endowments, Agency & Plant Funds

1. Endowment Funds:

Golden Rule: Regardless of the fund classification of the principal, earnings from the fund are unrestricted (unless the donor explicitly restricted their purpose!).

Endowment Classification Fund Classification of Principal
Pure or Regular Endowment Permanently Restricted (Principal untouchable; only earnings can be used).

2. Agency Funds: Pass-through funds where the NPO acts strictly as a custodian, agent, or steward with specific beneficiaries or recipients. Not a revenue! Must be accounted as a Liability.

3. Plant Funds: Only disclosed in the notes to financial statements, analogous to appropriated retained earnings in corporate accounting.

  • 1. Statement of Financial Position (SFP): Presents assets, liabilities, and net assets specifically categorized into Unrestricted, Temporarily Restricted, and Permanently Restricted.

    • Program Expenses: Result in distribution of goods/services to beneficiaries in pursuit of primary mission.
    • Support Expenses: All other non-program overhead (fundraising, general admin, membership development).
  • 4. Statement of Cash Flows: Follows standard cash flow classification with special donor add-ons:
    • Unrestricted Contribution Revenue: Operating Cash Flow
    • Restricted Contribution Revenue: Financing Cash Flow
  • 5. Notes to Financial Statements: Comprehensive explanatory disclosures.

Topic 12: Government Accounting Framework & Key Agencies

Definition

📖 Definition of Government Accounting

The process of analyzing, recording, classifying, summarizing, and communicating all transactions involving the receipt and disposition of government funds and property, and interpreting the results thereof.

The Government Budget: The financial plan of the government for a given period, showing what resources are, and how they will be generated and used over the period. It encompasses the income, expenditures, and sources of borrowings of the National Government that are used to achieve national objectives, strategies, and programs.

Constitutional Body

⚖️ 1. Commission on Audit (COA)

  • Holds exclusive authority to promulgate accounting and auditing rules and regulations for the government (Sec. 2(2), Art. IX-D, Philippine Constitution).
  • Keeps the general accounts of the government, consolidates financial reports, and submits the Annual Financial Report of government agencies to the President and the Congress not later than September 30.
  • Mandated the GAM: Prescribed the Government Accounting Manual (GAM) for National Government Agencies, effective January 1, 2016.
  • GAM superseded the New Government Accounting System (NGAS), which had been effective since June 18, 2002.
  • Prepared in accordance with the Philippine Public Sector Accounting Standards (PPSAS) on an Accrual Basis, unless not practicable.
The 3 Volumes of GAM:
  • Volume 1: Accounting Policies, Standards and Guidelines
  • Volume 2: Accounting Books, Registries, Records, Forms, and Reports
  • Volume 3: The Revised Chart of Accounts
Executive Agencies

💼 2. DBM, 3. BTr & 4. NGAs

2. Department of Budget and Management (DBM):

Formulates and implements the National Budget. Exercises control over expenditure programs, releases allotments, and issues Notice of Cash Allocation (NCA).

3. Bureau of the Treasury (BTr):

Functions under the Department of Finance (DOF) as the Cash Custodian of the Government. In charge of government fund receipts and disbursements. Maintains the Registry of NCA (RENCA) and monitors bank accounts/releases.

4. National Government Agencies (NGAs):

Maintains and keeps the agency's current accounts, accounting books, and budget registries. Submits monthly and quarterly Budget Accountability Reports (BARs) to DBM and COA.

📅

The Budget Process (Budget Cycle - 4 Phases)

Phase 1

📝 1. Budget Preparation

  • DBM issues the Budget Call.
  • Agencies hold Budget Hearings with DBM.
  • DBM consolidates and crafts the National Expenditure Plan (NEP) and presents it to the President.
  • Once approved by the President and Cabinet, the DBM submits the President's Budget (Approved NEP) to Congress within 30 days before the opening of the regular session.
Phase 2

⚖️ 2. Budget Legislation

  • House Deliberation: Must start exclusively in the lower chamber (House of Representatives) through a General Appropriations Bill (GAB).
  • Senate Deliberation: Senate crafts its own GAB version.
  • Bicameral Conference Committee: Resolves differences, producing the Harmonized GAB.
  • Ratification & Enrollment: Passed by both chambers.
  • Presidential Enactment: Enacted into the General Appropriations Act (GAA).
  • Unsigned within 30 days: Automatically deemed a law!
  • Presidential Veto: May be overturned by a 2/3 vote of all members of Congress.
  • If no GAA enacted: The prior year's budget is automatically adopted as a Reenacted Budget.
Phase 3

🚀 3. Budget Execution

  • Agencies submit Budget Execution Documents (BEDs) to DBM.
  • DBM prepares the Allotment Release Program (ARP), authorizing agencies to incur and enter into obligations.
  • DBM issues the Notice of Cash Allocation (NCA), a disbursement authority to government agencies authorizing cash withdrawals from modified disbursement system (MDS) banks.
Phase 4

📊 4. Budget Accountability

  • Occurs simultaneously (together) with Budget Execution!
  • Agencies submit monthly & quarterly Budget Accountability Reports (BARs) reviewed by DBM and COA.
  • Agency heads conduct an Annual Budget Performance Assessment Review (BPAR).
  • COA Audit: Comprehensive post-audit and financial compliance review.
High Yield Distinctions

🔑 Appropriation vs. Allotment vs. Obligation

🚨
CRITICAL RULE: "No incurring of obligations is allowed before receiving allotment."
Term Legal & Accounting Meaning Authorizing Party / Action
Appropriation A legislative authorization/law directing the payment of goods and services out of government funds under specified conditions. Passed by Congress (Legislative Branch) as the GAA.
Allotment An authorization issued by DBM to an agency allowing it to incur obligations within a specified amount that is within the legislative appropriation. Issued by DBM (Obligational Authority).
Obligation An act of a duly authorized agency official which binds the government to the immediate or eventual payment of a sum of money. Incurred by the Government Agency (via signed contracts/purchase orders).

Internal Control

🗂️ Budget Registries

Registries serve as the internal control mechanisms of the government budget to prevent overspending:

  • RAPAL: Registry of Appropriations and Allotments (Monitors appropriations and allotments received).
  • RAOD: Registry of Allotments, Obligations, and Disbursements (Tracks allotments, obligations incurred, and actual disbursements). Maintained by class:

  • RANCA: Registry of Allotments and Notice of Cash Allocation (Tracks NCA received and checks issued against it).

🔄 Reinsurance & Retrocession

1. Reinsurance Contract: A contract wherein the reinsurer agrees to compensate another insurer (cedant) for losses on one or more contracts issued by the cedant.

2. Retrocession Contract: A contract wherein the retrocessionaire agrees to compensate another insurer (reinsurer) for losses on one or more contracts issued by the reinsurer.

Recognition

⏰ When to Recognize Contracts

An entity shall recognize a group of insurance contracts it issues from the EARLIEST of the following dates:

  • 1. Beginning of coverage period of the group of contracts;
  • 2. Date when the first payment becomes due (or when the first payment is actually made, if no due date); or
  • 3. When a group of onerous contracts becomes onerous (if facts and circumstances indicate onerousness).

PFRS 17 Scope & Key Accounting Principles

Applicability

🎯 Scope of PFRS 17

✅ PFRS 17 APPLIES to:
  • Insurance contracts (including direct insurance issued)
  • Reinsurance contracts (both issued and held)
  • Investment contracts with discretionary participation features (if entity also issues insurance contracts)
❌ PFRS 17 DOES NOT APPLY to:
  • Financial instruments under PFRS 9
  • Product warranties issued by manufacturers, dealers, or retailers (PAS 37 / PFRS 15)
  • Contingent consideration in a business combination (PFRS 3)
  • Contingent contractual rights or obligations
  • Financial guarantee contracts (unless entity explicitly elected PFRS 17)
  • Employer's assets and liabilities under employee benefit plans (PAS 19)
  • Direct insurance contracts that the entity holds (as a policyholder)
Key Principles

🔑 Essential Mandates under PFRS 17

  • No Offsetting of Reinsurance: Reinsurance assets (net contractual rights) must NEVER be offset against reinsurance liabilities.
  • Annual Liability Adequacy Test: Mandatory annual assessment of the adequacy of recognized insurance liabilities based on current reviews of future cash flows.
  • Impairment Tests: Must perform regular impairment testing for insurance and reinsurance contracts held.
  • No Catastrophe Accounting: Provisions for future claims relating to possible future catastrophes (catastrophe or equalization reserves) are NO LONGER recognized!
  • Separation of Components (Unbundling): Embedded derivatives, investment components, and distinct performance obligations (goods or non-insurance services) must be separated from an insurance contract and accounted under relevant standards.
  • Aggregation & Portfolios: Assign contracts to portfolios of similar risks and managed together. Divide each portfolio into Three (3) Groups:
    • (a) Contracts that are onerous at inception;
    • (b) Contracts with no significant possibility of becoming onerous subsequently; and
    • (c) Any other remaining contracts.
  • Changes in Accounting Policy: Allowed ONLY IF the financial statements present information that is:
    • More relevant and no less reliable, OR
    • More reliable and no less relevant.
  • Modifications: If terms are modified substantially, derecognize the original contract and recognize the modified contract as a new contract!

Methods of Measuring Unearned Premium Reserve

Fixed Percentage

📊 1. Fixed Percentage Method

Measures the unearned premium reserve by applying a statutory or specific percentage to the total premiums written in each class of business insurance.

💡
Typically used as a regulatory rule of thumb or baseline reserve across entire blocks of insurance policies.
Time Apportionment

⏳ 2. Time Apportionment Method

Measures unearned premium reserve for both annual and non-annual policies based on elapsed time and unexpired risk coverage periods.

Subdivided into 3 specific mathematical applications for practice and regulatory reporting:

Daily Pro-Rata

📅 1/365th Method

Measures unearned premiums on an exact daily pro-rata basis in respect of the unexpired portions of respective insurance policies at the end of each period (based on 365 days in a year).

Unearned Premium = Gross Premium × (Unexpired Days / 365)
Semi-Monthly

🌓 1/24th Method

A practical simplification of time apportionment, applicable ONLY to one-year term insurance policies.

  • Assumes premium payments are spread evenly twice a month (12 × 2 = 24 intervals).
  • Treats policies written in any month as if issued in the middle of each semi-monthly block.
Quarterly

🌗 1/8th Method

Based on the general assumption that premiums are spread evenly over the quarter and the average date of all policies written is in the middle of the quarter.

  • Assumes premiums are accounted twice every quarter (4 quarters × 2 = 8 intervals).
  • Widely used for quarterly policy portfolios.

Topic 14.2: Service Concession Arrangements (PFRIC 12 & PPSAS 32)

Definition & Terms

🌉 What is a Service Concession Arrangement?

An arrangement wherein the Grantor (usually the Public Sector or Government) contracts with an Operator (usually a Private Entity) to develop, upgrade, operate, and maintain the grantor's infrastructure assets (e.g., tollways, bridges, airports, water supply, power plants).

🛣️ Built-Operate-Transfer (BOT) 🔄 Rehabilitate-Operate-Transfer (ROT) 🤝 Public-Private Partnership (PPP)
Grantor Accounting

🏛️️ Grantor (Public Sector / Government)

Governed by PPSAS 32 (Service Concession Arrangements: Grantor). The government agency recognizes the asset and an associated liability under either of two models:

1. Financial Liability Model (FLM):

Recognized when the grantor has an unconditional obligation to pay cash or another financial asset to the operator.

2. Grant of a Right to the Operator Model (GORTOM):

Recognized as Deferred Revenue (liability) when the grantor gives the operator the right to earn revenue from third-party users of the asset.

Operator Accounting

🏢 Operator (Private Entity - PFRIC 12)

Governed by PFRIC 12. Crucial rule: PFRIC 12 does not specify new general accounting rules, but determines WHICH existing standard applies to the operator:

💡
The operator does NOT recognize the infrastructure as PPE because it does not control the asset. Instead, it recognizes either an Intangible Asset or a Financial Asset!
