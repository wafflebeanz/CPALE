Topic 6: Business Combinations (PFRS 3) Basics & Acquisition Method

PFRS 3 Scope

📖 Scope & Core Concept

A Business Combination is a transaction or other event in which an acquirer obtains control of one or more businesses. Governed by PFRS 3 (Business Combinations), which mandates the Acquisition Method (referred to as the Purchase Method under PFRS for SMEs).

💡
Crucial Control Test: Control exists when the investor has exposure or rights to variable returns from its involvement with the investee and has the ability to affect those returns through its power over the relevant activities (PFRS 10).
Mandatory Steps

🐾 The 4 Steps of the Acquisition Method

  • Step 1: Identify the Acquirer → The combining entity that obtains control of the acquiree (controls the relevant activities).
  • Step 2: Determine the Acquisition Date → The date on which the acquirer officially obtains control of the acquiree.
  • Step 3: Recognize & Measure Identifiable Assets, Liabilities & NCI → Measured at their Fair Values at the acquisition date.
  • Step 4: Recognize & Measure Goodwill or Gain from Bargain Purchase:
    • If Total Consideration or Aggregate is greater than FVINA → Goodwill (Asset).
    • If Total Consideration or Aggregate is less than FVINA → Gain from Bargain Purchase (credited to Profit/Loss immediately).

Net Asset Acquisition (Statutory Merger / Consolidation) & ARC

Consideration & FVINA

💰 Elements of Consideration & FVINA

In a Net Asset Acquisition, the acquiree corporation is dissolved, leaving a surviving acquirer corporation.

Consideration FormMeasurement Basis
CashAt Face Value
Non-Cash AssetsAt Fair Value at Acquisition Date
Debt Securities (Bonds)At Fair Value (net of bond issue costs if amortized cost)
Equity Securities (Shares)At Fair Value (Par + Share Premium net of stock issue costs)
Contingent ConsiderationAt Fair Value. If no direct FV is given, measure to extent probable:
Payment × % Probability × PV Factor (if applicable)

Deriving FVINA (Fair Value of Identifiable Net Assets):

Method 1: Fair Value of Acquiree's Assets − Fair Value of Acquiree's Liabilities

Method 2 (Adjusting Book Value):
FVINA = Book Value of Acquiree's Net Assets + Undervalued Assets − Overvalued Assets − Undervalued Liabilities + Overvalued Liabilities − Previous Goodwill of Acquiree (if any)
🚨
Golden Rules: Goodwill's fair value is ALWAYS ZERO! The fair value of the Acquirer is COMPLETELY IRRELEVANT in determining Goodwill or FVINA!
Transaction Costs

💸 Acquisition-Related Costs (ARC) & Issue Costs

General Rule for ARC: Expensed outright as incurred (deducted from Retained Earnings).

Cost TypeMandatory Accounting Treatment
Bond Issue Costs • Bonds @ Amortized Cost: Contra-Liability (deduction from Bonds Payable Carrying Amount).
• Bonds @ FVPL: Expensed Outright (P/L).
Stock Issue Costs Contra-Equity Account, deducted strictly in order of priority:
  1. Priority 1: Share Premium – Related Issuance
  2. Priority 2: Share Premium – Previous Issuances
  3. Priority 3: Retained Earnings (only if Share Premium is completely exhausted!)
Direct Costs under SMEs Capitalized as part of the cost of acquisition under PFRS for SMEs (contrary to full PFRS).
⚖
Cash & Liability Impact at Date of Acquisition (DOA):
  • ARC if Paid: Direct reduction to Total Assets at DOA.
  • ARC if Unpaid: Direct addition to Total Liabilities at DOA.

The Measurement Period & Contingent Consideration

1-Year Window

⏰ Requisites & Adjustment Location

The Measurement Period allows the acquirer to adjust provisional amounts recognized at the acquisition date.

Requisites to Qualify:
  • Must be within one (1) year from the Date of Acquisition; AND
  • Must be due to information existing since the Date of Acquisition.
Common Exclusions (Never qualify for measurement period adjustment):
  • Subsequent changes in earnings or profit
  • Fluctuations in share prices
  • Achieving post-acquisition milestones in Research & Development (R&D)

Outside Measurement PeriodAdjust Retained Earnings (Profit or Loss)

Stock Acquisition, NCI Measurement & Goodwill Allocation

Control Structure

🏰 Stock Acquisition Framework

Results in a Parent (Acquirer) and Subsidiary (Acquiree) relationship, recognizing a Non-Controlling Interest (NCI) when the parent acquires less than 100% of voting shares.

Aggregate Amount = Total Consideration + NCI + Fair Value of Previously Held Equity Instrument (FV-PHEI)
Goodwill / (Gain on BP) = Aggregate Amount − FVINA
NCI Valuation

🧸 Measurement of NCI at Date of Acquisition

Option 1 Fair Value (Assumed if Silent)
  • Must NOT be below Proportionate Share.
  • Given Fair Value OR Assumed Fair Value:
    Assumed FV of NCI = [(Total Consideration − Control Premium) / Controlling Interest %] × NCI %
  • Results in Full Goodwill (allocated to both CI and NCI).
Option 2 Proportionate Share (Minimum Amount)
  • NCI at Proportionate Share = FVINA × NCI %
  • Results in Partial Goodwill (allocated 100% to CI; NCI has zero goodwill).
💡
Gain from Bargain Purchase Rule: If the transaction results in a Gain from Bargain Purchase, it is ALWAYS 100% allocated to the Controlling Interest (CI)!
Step Acquisitions & SMEs

🔍 Measuring FV-PHEI & SME Differences

FV of Previously Held Equity Instrument (FV-PHEI):

In a step acquisition, the previous investment is remeasured to Fair Value at DOA:

Assumed FV-PHEI = [Total Consideration / % Newly Acquired] × PHEI %
(No need to consider or deduct the control premium!)
Remeasurement Gain / (Loss) in P/L = FV-PHEI at DOA − Carrying Amount of PHEI at DOA
Key Differences for PFRS for SMEs:
  • Only Partial Goodwill is allowed (Full Goodwill is strictly prohibited!).
  • NCI is ALWAYS measured at Proportionate Share.

Essential Consolidated Formulas: Sales, COGS, Inventory & Gross Profit

💡
Master Consolidation Rationale: The consolidated group is treated as a single economic entity. All intra-group transfers (intercompany sales, purchases, and unrealized markup) must be eliminated so financial statements reflect only transactions with external third parties!
Formula 1

🏷️ 1. Consolidated Sales (Conso Sales)

Consolidated Sales = Parent Sales + Subsidiary Sales − Intercompany Sales
  • Intercompany Sales: The total billing price of all merchandise sold between parent and subsidiary during the period (both upstream and downstream).
  • Why Deducted? To eliminate internal double counting. Only sales to outside third parties represent genuine consolidated revenue.
Formula 2

📦 2. Consolidated Inventory (Conso Inventory)

Consolidated Inventory = Parent Ending Inventory + Subsidiary Ending Inventory − UPEI + Unamortized Excess FV of Inventory
  • UPEI (Unrealized Profit in Ending Inventory): The markup on intercompany goods still sitting in the buyer's warehouse at year-end. Deducted to reduce inventory back to original consolidated cost.
  • Unamortized Excess FV: Added if acquired subsidiary inventory had a fair value higher than book value at DOA and remains unsold to outsiders at year-end.
Formula 3

📉 3. Consolidated Cost of Goods Sold (Conso COGS)

Consolidated COGS = Parent COGS + Subsidiary COGS − Intercompany Sales + UPEI − RPBI + Amortization of Undervalued Inventory − Amortization of Overvalued Inventory
ComponentEffect on Conso COGSAccounting Reason
Less: Intercompany Sales − Deduct Reverses the duplicate cost recorded upon intra-group transfer.
Add: UPEI + Add Ending inventory was overstated by markup; lower ending inventory increases COGS!
Less: RPBI (Beg UPEI) − Deduct Beginning inventory was overstated by prior year's markup; lower beginning inventory decreases COGS!
Add: Undervalued Inv. Amort. + Add Additional acquisition cost recognized when acquiree's inventory is sold to external parties.
Formula 4

🍰 4. Consolidated Gross Profit (Conso GP)

Can be calculated using Two (2) Equivalent Perspectives:

Method A: Direct Top-Down Approach
Consolidated Gross Profit = Consolidated Sales − Consolidated COGS
Method B: Reconciliation from Separate Gross Profits
Consolidated GP = Parent GP + Subsidiary GP − UPEI + RPBI − Amortization of Undervalued Inventory + Amortization of Overvalued Inventory
💡
Notice that Intercompany Sales cancels out between Sales and COGS, proving that Method A and Method B always yield the exact same result!

Transaction Direction

🔀 Downstream vs. Upstream

Downstream (Parent → Subsidiary):

The Parent is the selling entity. All unrealized profits/losses and subsequent realizations are allocated 100% to the Controlling Interest (CI). NCI is completely unaffected (0%)!

Upstream (Subsidiary → Parent):

The Subsidiary is the selling entity. All unrealized profits/losses and subsequent realizations are shared proportionally between CI and NCI based on their respective percentages (CI % and NCI %).

Topic 11: Not-for-Profit Organizations (NPO) Basics

Fundamentals

🧸 Basic Characteristics of NPOs

Operated strictly for the benefit of society as a whole (other than for profit):

  • Cultural & Charitable
  • Civic & Social
  • Educational & Religious
💡
Key Distinctions:
  • Receives gratuitous considerations (donations), but can still receive onerous considerations (e.g., tuition, hospital bills, cafeteria sales).
  • No ownership interest: No stock shares, dividends, or residual equity holders.
Revenue Types

💎 Major Sources of Revenue

1 Onerous Considerations (Exchange)
  • Regular Sale of Goods or Services: Always classified as Unrestricted.
  • Investment Income: Usually classified as Unrestricted (unless the donor stipulated that earnings are restricted for a designated purpose).
2 Gratuitous Considerations (Non-Exchange)
  • Donations (termed Contribution Revenue).
  • May be classified as Unrestricted, Temporarily Restricted, or Permanently Restricted depending on donor instructions.
Scope & Rules

📚 Accounting Framework & Categories

Accounting Standards: Accrual basis under:

  • PFRS with the Conceptual Framework
  • US GAAP: SFAS 116 (Contributions Received and Made) and SFAS 117 (Financial Statements of NPOs)

4 Common Categories of NPOs:

  • 11.1. Voluntary Health and Welfare Organizations (VHWO): Primary source of revenues is exclusively from gratuitous considerations (donations from public).
  • 11.2. Hospitals and Other Health Care Organizations
  • 11.3. Colleges and Universities
  • 11.4. Other NPOs: Churches, Museums, Fraternities, Political Parties, etc.
Net Asset Classes

🍰 Composition of Net Assets or Funds

In NPO accounting, net assets take the place of owner's equity and are strictly grouped by donor restrictions:

🍦 Unrestricted Net Assets

  • No donor-imposed restrictions at all.
  • Includes resources whose use is within the Board of Trustees' (BOT) discretion.
  • Board-designated funds (quasi-endowments, internal reserves) are strictly Unrestricted because only external donors can impose valid restrictions!

⏳ Temporarily Restricted

  • Subject to donor-imposed restrictions as to:
    • Time: Available only in future periods or term endowment.
    • Purpose: For a specific project/asset purchase.
    • Happening of an event: Event occurrence.
  • Release Mechanism: Once the time elapses, the purpose is accomplished, or the event occurs, the amount is reclassified (transferred) to Unrestricted Net Assets.

🔒 Permanently Restricted

  • Subject to donor-imposed restrictions that do not expire and cannot be removed by the NPO.
  • Usually invested (Pure Endowment): The principal must remain untouchable in perpetuity, and only the investment interest/dividends are usable.

Topic 13.4: Backflush Costing System & Just-In-Time (JIT) System

JIT Philosophy

🏭 Just-In-Time (JIT) Core Pillars

A philosophy that strives for zero waste and maximum operational efficiency:

  • Few, High-Reliability Suppliers: Long-term contracts ensuring zero delivery defects.
  • "Pull" System: Production is initiated strictly upon receipt of actual customer orders.
  • Low or Zero Inventory Levels: Minimizes holding and storage costs for Raw Materials, WIP, and Finished Goods.
  • Multi-Skilled / Cross-Trained Employees: Operators perform production, maintenance, and quality inspection.
  • Synergy with Backflush Costing: Streamlines and eliminates voluminous, sequential journal entries!
Backflush Basics

🍰 Backflush Cost Accumulation Procedures

Eliminates separate tracking through Work-in-Process inventory and delays recording until specific trigger points:

  • Standard Costing Adherence: Uses predetermined standard costs for DM and Conversion Costs (DL + Applied OH).
  • Insignificant Variances: Any cost variances are deemed immaterial and closed directly to Cost of Goods Sold (COGS).
Master Manufacturing Flow Formulas:
Cost of Goods Manufactured (COGM) = DM Used + DL Actual + OH Applied
Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) = COGM × (Units Sold / Units Produced)
Ending Finished Goods Inventory (FG) = COGM − COGS = COGM × (Units Unsold / Units Produced)

Topic 13.5: Activity-Based Costing (ABC System)

Traditional vs. ABC

⚖️ Allocation of Costs: Traditional vs. ABC

Traditional Costing: Uses a single plant-wide or departmental volume-based cost driver (e.g., Direct Labor Hours or Machine Hours). Distorts cost by subsidizing low-volume, complex products with high-volume, simple products.

Activity-Based Costing (ABC): Uses multiple cost pools and diverse cost drivers reflecting actual consumption of activities, resulting in far more accurate production costs.

💡
ABC is applied to homogenous cost pools where costs in each pool share a single causal driver.
Activity Hierarchy

🪜 The 4 Activity Levels

  • 1. Unit-Level Activities: Performed each time a unit is produced (e.g., machine electricity, direct labor, cutting, assembly).
  • 2. Batch-Level Activities: Performed each time a batch is processed (e.g., machine setups, material handling, purchase ordering, batch inspections).
  • 3. Product-Sustaining Activities: Performed to support an entire product line regardless of batches (e.g., product redesign, engineering change orders, testing prototypes).
  • 4. Facility-Sustaining Activities: Common factory-wide overhead (e.g., factory building rent, security, plant manager salary, general insurance).
PDR & Application

📐 Determination of Cost Pool Rates & Applied Overhead

Step 1: Predetermined Rate (PDR) per Cost Driver
PDR = Budgeted Overhead Cost of Pool / Budgeted Activity Level of Driver
Step 2: Total Applied Overhead to a Product
Applied Overhead = Sum of (PDR per Cost Driver × Actual Activity)
📌
Alternative Calculation (When Budgeted Activity is Not Given):
Applied Overhead = Specific Activity Overhead × (Actual Product Activity / Total Activity of All Products)

Topic 13.6: Accounting for Joint Products & By-Products

Core Terminology

📖 Joint Production Fundamentals

Split-Off Point: The point in the joint manufacturing process where individual products become separately identifiable.
Joint Costs: Costs incurred prior to split-off. Must generally be allocated to main products only (zero to incidental products).
By-Products: Incidental products resulting from joint production with relatively minor total sales value.
4 Allocation Methods

📐 Allocation of Joint Costs (Without By-Products)

MethodFormula / BasisKey CPALE Characteristics
A. Physical Measures Physical output (Units, Liters, Kilos, Board Feet) "Unchanging Yardstick." Ignores economic revenue-generating ability; cannot be used if products have different physical units.
B. Sales Value @ Split-Off
Selling Price @ Split-Off × Units Produced
 Allocates costs based on market value at split-off. Applicable only if a market price exists at split-off.
C. NRV @ Split-Off
(Selling Price @ Split-Off − Disposal Cost @ Split-Off) × Units Produced
 Takes into account separable disposal/selling expenses that would be incurred if sold at split-off.
D. Approximated NRV
(Final SP after FP − FP Cost − Disposal Cost after FP) × Units Produced
 Hypothetical / Estimated NRV. Applied when products require Further Processing (FP) before sale.
🚨
Golden Units Rule:
  • In allocating joint costs → ALWAYS use Units Produced!
  • In determining disposal cost / selling expenses → ALWAYS use Units Sold!
📦
Determining Cost of Goods Sold (COGS):
  • If sold at split-off: Allocated Joint Cost × (Units Sold / Units Produced)
  • If sold after further processing: [Allocated Joint Cost × (Units Sold / Units Produced)] + Further Processing Costs of units sold!
📦

Methods of Accounting for By-Products

Production Unrecognized

💰 A. Net Realized Value Method (Recognized When Sold)

By-product is recognized ONLY when sold. No joint costs are allocated upon production. Measured at NRV of Units Sold.

Presentation OptionFinancial Statement Effect
1. As Additional Sales Revenue ↑ Increases Gross Profit
2. As Other Income Does NOT affect Gross Profit (shown below operating income)
3. As Reduction in Joint Cost (or COGS) ↓ Reduces Allocated Joint Costs to Main Products
Production Recognized

📦 B. Net Realizable Value Method (Recognized When Produced)

By-product is recognized at production and inventoriable. Joint costs are allocated to or reduced by the by-product.

I. Standard NRV Method:

By-product is measured at NRV of Units Produced = Units Produced × (Final Sales Value − Separable Costs − Disposal Costs). This amount directly offsets the Joint Costs before allocation to main products.

II. Reversal Cost Method:

Works backward from estimated final sales value of produced by-product − Normal Profit Margin − Marketing/Admin Expenses − Separable Further Processing Costs = Credited to Joint Cost!

Topic 13.7: Service Department Cost Allocation Methods

3 Master Methods

📑 Direct vs. Step-Down vs. Reciprocal (Algebraic)

Service Departments (SD) provide internal support to other departments. Their costs must be allocated to Production Departments (PD) for product costing:

Allocation Method Core Operational Principle Inter-Service Department Support
1. Direct Method Service departments allocate their costs solely to Production Departments (PDs). IGNORED Completely ignores mutual services rendered between service departments.
2. Step-Down (Sequential) Method Allocates sequentially based on a benefit-provided ranking. Rank 1 SD allocates to other SDs and all PDs. Subsequent SDs allocate only to lower SDs and PDs. PARTIAL One-directional only! Once an SD's costs are allocated, no costs can be allocated back to it.
3. Reciprocal (Algebraic) Method All service departments allocate to both Service and Production Departments using simultaneous equations. COMPLETE Fully recognizes reciprocal services; total cost of each SD includes what it receives from other SDs.

Topic 14.1: Accounting for Insurance Contracts by Insurers (PFRS 17)

PFRS 17 Core

📜 Definition of Insurance Contract

A contract under which the insurer accepts significant insurance risk from the policyholder by agreeing to compensate the policyholder if a specified uncertain future event adversely affects the policyholder.

💡
Insurance Risk: Risk other than financial risk, transferred from the holder of a contract to the issuer.
Layers of Risk

🔄 Reinsurance & Retrocession

1. Reinsurance Contract: A contract wherein the reinsurer agrees to compensate another insurer (cedant) for losses on one or more contracts issued by the cedant.

2. Retrocession Contract: A contract wherein the retrocessionaire agrees to compensate another insurer (reinsurer) for losses on one or more contracts issued by the reinsurer.

Recognition

⏰ When to Recognize Contracts

An entity shall recognize a group of insurance contracts it issues from the EARLIEST of the following dates:

  • 1. Beginning of coverage period of the group of contracts;
  • 2. Date when the first payment becomes due (or when the first payment is actually made, if no due date); or
  • 3. When a group of onerous contracts becomes onerous (if facts and circumstances indicate onerousness).

PFRS 17 Scope & Key Accounting Principles

Applicability

🎯 Scope of PFRS 17

✅ PFRS 17 APPLIES to:
  • Insurance contracts (including direct insurance issued)
  • Reinsurance contracts (both issued and held)
  • Investment contracts with discretionary participation features
❌ PFRS 17 DOES NOT APPLY to:
  • Financial instruments under PFRS 9
  • Product warranties (PAS 37 / PFRS 15)
  • Contingent consideration in business combinations (PFRS 3)
  • Financial guarantee contracts (unless entity explicitly elected PFRS 17)
  • Employer's assets and liabilities under employee benefit plans (PAS 19)
  • Direct insurance contracts that the entity holds (as a policyholder)
Key Principles

🔑 Essential Mandates under PFRS 17

  • No Offsetting of Reinsurance: Reinsurance assets must NEVER be offset against reinsurance liabilities.
  • Annual Liability Adequacy Test: Mandatory annual review of recognized insurance liabilities against projected future cash flows.
  • Impairment Tests: Regular testing for insurance and reinsurance contracts held.
  • No Catastrophe Accounting: Catastrophe or equalization reserves are NO LONGER recognized!
  • Separation of Components: Embedded derivatives, investment components, and distinct goods/services must be separated (unbundled).
  • Portfolios & Aggregation: Divide into: (a) Onerous at inception; (b) No significant possibility of becoming onerous; (c) Remaining contracts.
  • Modifications: Substantial modifications require derecognition of original contract and recognition as a new contract!

Methods of Measuring Unearned Premium Reserve

Fixed Percentage

📊 1. Fixed Percentage Method

Measures unearned premium reserve by applying a statutory or specific percentage to total premiums written in each class of business insurance.

Time Apportionment

⏳ 2. Time Apportionment Method

Measures reserve for both annual and non-annual policies based on elapsed time and unexpired risk coverage periods.

Daily Pro-Rata

📅 1/365th Method

Daily pro-rata basis for unexpired portions based on 365 days.

Unearned Premium = Gross Premium × (Unexpired Days / 365)
Semi-Monthly

🌓 1/24th Method

Simplification for 1-year term policies assuming payments occur twice a month (24 equal intervals).

Quarterly

🌗 1/8th Method

Assumes premiums are spread twice every quarter (4 quarters × 2 = 8 intervals).
