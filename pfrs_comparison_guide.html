SEC / FRSC Reporting Framework

Philippine Financial Reporting Standards Comparison

An authoritative guide differentiating accounting treatments across Micro Entities, Small Entities (SEs), SMEs, and Full PFRS/IFRS based on SEC guidelines.

1. Entity Classification & Thresholds

M

Micro Entities

  • Threshold: Assets or Liabilities below ₱3,000,000.
  • Accountability: Not publicly accountable.
  • Framework: Option to use PFRS for SEs OR Income Tax Basis.
SE

Small Entities

  • Threshold: Assets or Liabilities between ₱3M and ₱100M.
  • Accountability: Not publicly accountable.
  • Framework: Mandatory PFRS for SEs (unless exempt).
SME

SMEs

  • Threshold: Assets ₱3M - ₱350M OR Liabilities ₱3M - ₱250M.
  • Accountability: Not publicly accountable.
  • Framework: PFRS for SMEs (default).
Full

Tier 1 / Full PFRS

  • Threshold: Exceeds SME limits. No size limit if publicly accountable.
  • Accountability: Publicly Accountable Entities (listed, banks, insurance).
  • Framework: Full PFRS / IFRS mandatory.

3. Transition Rules & SEC Exemptions

Threshold Breaches

What happens if an entity crosses the size thresholds (floor or ceiling)?

  • If a breach occurs at year-end, the entity must transition the next year.
  • Ceiling breached: Move up to the higher framework (e.g., SME to Full IFRS).
  • Floor breached: Move down to the simpler framework.
  • Crucial Condition: The transition is only required if the event causing the breach is "significant and continuing".
  • General Rule: A change of 20% or more of total assets or liabilities is considered significant.

First-Time Adoption Reconciliations

  • Small Entities (SEs): Simplified transition reliefs. Must prepare opening Statement of Financial Position, but detailed reconciliations like SMEs/Full are generally not required.
  • SMEs & Full PFRS: Must prepare an opening SoFP. Required to present reconciliations of (a) Equity under previous basis to the new basis at transition date and latest period end, and (b) Profit or loss under previous basis to new basis.

Key SEC Exemptions

An entity that fits the size criteria for SMEs or SEs may be exempt from mandatory adoption (and often pushed toward Full PFRS or allowed to retain it) if it meets specific criteria, such as:

  • Subsidiary of a parent reporting under Full IFRS.
  • Subsidiary of a foreign parent moving toward Full IFRS.
  • Has concrete plans for an IPO within 2 years (SME exemption).
  • Part of a group as a JV or associate reporting under Full IFRS.
  • Branch office of a foreign entity reporting under Full IFRS.
  • Has short-term projections of breaching quantitative thresholds (significant & continuing).
