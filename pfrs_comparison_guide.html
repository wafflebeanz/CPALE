PFRS Comparison Guide

A comprehensive comparison between Full PFRS, PFRS for SMEs, and PFRS for SEs based on the Philippine Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) and the Financial Reporting Standards Council (FRSC) tiered framework.

Framework Tiers

  • Tier 1
    Large / Public Interest Entities: Use Full PFRS. (Generally, Total Assets > ₱350M or Total Liabilities > ₱250M)
  • Tier 2
    Medium-sized Entities: Use PFRS for SMEs. (Total Assets ₱100M - ₱350M or Total Liabilities ₱100M - ₱250M)
  • Tier 3
    Small Entities: Use PFRS for SEs. (Total Assets ₱3M - ₱100M or Total Liabilities ₱3M - ₱100M)

2. Detailed Accounting Treatments

A. Asset Valuation

Investment Property: Under Full PFRS, companies have a free policy choice between fair value and cost. SMEs must use fair value if it can be determined without undue cost or effort, defaulting to cost if it cannot. Small Entities (SEs) are entirely relieved of fair value assessments for investment properties and must strictly use the Cost-Depreciation-Impairment model.

Biological Assets (Agriculture): Full PFRS requires Fair Value less costs to sell. PFRS for SMEs requires Fair Value only if readily determinable without undue cost; otherwise, cost model. PFRS for SEs strictly uses the Cost model.

B. Intangibles and Goodwill

Amortization: Full PFRS recognizes that some intangibles (like established brand names) have an indefinite useful life and are only checked for impairment. To simplify accounting, both SMEs and SEs assume all intangible assets and goodwill lose value over time. If management cannot reliably estimate the useful life, they are forced to amortize it over 10 years.

C. Leases (For Lessees)

Treatment: Full PFRS (specifically PFRS 16) eliminated the concept of "Operating Leases" for lessees; almost all leases go onto the balance sheet as an asset and a liability. PFRS for SMEs and SEs retain the old rules (equivalent to the old PAS 17), meaning short-term or standard rental agreements can just be expensed straight to the income statement (Operating Lease) without inflating the balance sheet.

D. Income Taxes

Deferred Taxes: Calculating deferred tax assets and liabilities requires tracking the temporary differences between accounting profit and taxable profit, which is notoriously complex. While Full PFRS and SMEs must do this, PFRS for SEs offers a massive simplification: the Taxes Payable Method. SEs can simply record the income tax expense equal to what is actually owed to the BIR for that year, ignoring deferred taxes entirely.

E. Borrowing Costs & R&D

Capitalization: Full PFRS requires entities to capitalize interest/borrowing costs on loans used to build major assets, and to capitalize development costs for new products. This builds up the asset value on the balance sheet. SMEs and SEs follow a much simpler "expense immediately" rule. All interest and all R&D costs hit the income statement right away, which simplifies bookkeeping but can lower short-term profitability.

F. Employee Benefits

Retirement Accrual: Full PFRS requires hiring an independent actuary to calculate retirement obligations using the Projected Unit Credit method. SMEs should use this if it's not too costly, but can fall back to simpler methods. SEs have the easiest route: they can simply calculate their liability based on the minimum requirements of Philippine Republic Act No. 7641 (usually half a month's salary for every year of service) using management's best estimate, with no actuary required.

G. Financial Statement Presentation

Statements Required: While all frameworks require a Statement of Financial Position, Income Statement, and Cash Flows, SEs and SMEs that have no Other Comprehensive Income (OCI) items can present a single "Statement of Income and Retained Earnings" instead of separate Statements of Comprehensive Income and Changes in Equity.
